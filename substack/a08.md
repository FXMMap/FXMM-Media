# Order Flow, in Plain Words

*Candles show where price went. Order flow shows who pushed it there, and whether they're still pushing.*

[COVER IMAGE: https://fxmmap.github.io/FXMM-Media/substack/a08_cover.jpg]

Two candles can look identical and mean very different things. A strong red candle might be heavy, committed selling. Or it might be a few sellers pushing into a market where nobody wanted to buy for a moment. On the chart, you can't tell them apart.

Order flow is one way to tell the difference.

## Who was in a hurry

Every trade has a buyer and a seller. So "more buyers than sellers" isn't quite right. What changes is who was in a hurry.

Some traders wait with limit orders at a price they're willing to accept. Others cross the spread and take whatever price is available, because they want in or out now. Those are the aggressive orders, and they're the ones that move price.

Order flow tracks that urgency. The most common measure is **delta**: aggressive buying minus aggressive selling over a period. Positive delta means buyers were more urgent. Negative delta means sellers were.

## What delta adds to a chart

On its own, a candle tells you the result. Delta tells you the effort behind it. The useful moments are when the two disagree.

**Price falls, but selling dries up.** Each push lower comes with less aggressive selling than the one before. The move continues, but with less fuel. That doesn't mean it turns. It means the pressure behind it is fading.

[CHART IMAGE: https://fxmmap.github.io/FXMM-Media/substack/a08_chart.png]

**Heavy selling, but price barely moves.** Sellers are hitting hard, yet price holds. That usually means someone is absorbing it with large passive orders. Effort without result is information.

**Price breaks a level with weak delta.** The chart says breakout. The order flow says few were in a hurry to push it. Like a breakout on thin volume, it's a reason to look twice.

## Where the numbers come from

Spot FX has no central exchange, so there's no single public record of who traded aggressively. Order flow in your broker's feed only shows that broker's clients.

Desks measure it in markets where every trade and its side are recorded, which is part of the institutional data they pay for. Even then, it's a short-term layer. It describes the last minutes or hours, not the next week.

## How to use it as a retail trader

You don't need to read every tick. A simple routine:

1. **Start from a level, not from the order flow.** Decide in advance where you'd pay attention. Order flow is for when price gets there, not for finding trades.
2. **As price approaches, compare effort and result.** Is the move into the level getting stronger or weaker in delta?
3. **Watch for disagreement.** Fading pressure into a level, or heavy pressure that stops moving price, are the moments worth noting.
4. **Don't act on order flow alone.** It's noisy, fast and easy to over-read. One reading is a clue, not a decision.

## Order flow is one layer

Order flow answers a narrow question: right now, who is in a hurry? It doesn't tell you where the important levels are or which way the bigger forces lean.

That's why it works best with the other layers we've covered here: how large traders are positioned, where real volume has traded, and which way rate expectations are pulling. When price reaches a zone with that context behind it, and the pressure into it is fading, the level carries more weight than any single reading could give it.

## The takeaway

Candles are the result. Order flow is the effort. When effort and result stop agreeing, pay attention.
