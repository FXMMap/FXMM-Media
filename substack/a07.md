# Your Feed Only Shows You the Winners

*Trading content runs on screenshots of the best trades. Almost nobody posts the rest, and that quietly changes what "normal" looks like.*

[COVER IMAGE: https://fxmmap.github.io/FXMM-Media/substack/a07_cover.jpg]

Open any trading feed for five minutes and you'll see the same thing. A clean chart, an arrow, a green number. A trade that caught the whole move. Someone who "called it."

Now ask a simple question: where are the other trades?

Not the best one of the month. All of them. The ones that hit the stop. The ones that went nowhere for three days. The ones that were never taken because the setup didn't line up. They exist. They just don't get posted.

## The highlight reel isn't a lie. It's a sample.

Most of what you see in trading feeds isn't fake. The trade probably happened. The chart is probably real.

The problem is selection. People post what looks good, and what looks good is the rare trade that went perfectly. Platforms reward it, because a big green number gets more attention than a small red one. Over time, your feed fills up with the top one percent of outcomes, from thousands of traders, every single day.

No single post misleads you. The collection does. When everything you see is a highlight, highlights start to feel like the baseline.

## What that does to a normal trader

Compare your own month with your feed and it will always look worse. That's not because you're doing something wrong. It's because you're comparing your whole record with everyone else's best moments.

The consequences are predictable. Small, realistic targets start to feel unambitious. A month that ends slightly up feels like failure. A planned trade that loses feels like proof you're missing something. And the natural response is to reach for what the screenshots seem to show: bigger targets, more trades, more risk on the one that "looks like the one."

That's the same road that leads to the home run trade we wrote about a few weeks ago. The feed doesn't push you onto it directly. It just keeps showing you people who seem to be standing at the end of it.

## Who benefits from the highlight reel

As we argued in an earlier piece, many products around retail trading earn more when you're active. The highlight reel fits that model well.

A channel that posts winners keeps subscribers hoping. A course that shows a perfect trade sells the idea that it can be repeated on demand. A platform that celebrates big wins makes the next trade feel urgent. None of that requires bad intent. It only requires that winners get shared and losers don't, which is how social media already works.

What's missing in all of it is the denominator. One winning trade tells you nothing without the number of trades it came from, the risk on each, and the months that didn't make the feed.

## What a real record looks like

Professional desks don't judge a trader by the best trade. They look at the full record: how many decisions, how much risk on each, how losses were handled, and whether the process stayed the same on bad days and good ones.

An illustrative comparison. Trader A posts four big winners in a month and nothing else. Trader B shares every trade: fourteen in total, risk fixed on each, a few losses, a handful of modest wins, a month that ends slightly up. Trader A looks better in the feed. Only Trader B gives you anything you can actually learn from.

That's the part the highlight reel removes. Not the wins, but the context around them.

## A few habits that help

You can't change the feed. You can change how you read it:

1. **Ask for the denominator.** When you see a winning trade, ask how many trades it came from. If nobody says, treat it as one data point, not a pattern.
2. **Judge your month by process, not by screenshots.** Count how many of your trades started at a level you planned in advance.
3. **Keep your own full record.** Every trade, every stop, every trade you skipped. It's the only honest comparison you have.
4. **Mute what makes you rush.** If a feed regularly makes you want to size up or trade faster, it's costing you more than it's teaching you.

## The trade you don't see

The most useful information in trading is usually invisible: the trade that wasn't taken because nothing lined up. It never makes a screenshot, and it's often the best decision of the week.
